## What This Review Can and Cannot Judge

This is a source-checked review of Do Disciplinary Committee Members Dream of Succubi, not a claim of hands-on play. The available evidence includes the official Steam and Kagura pages, the Japanese DLsite listing, a developer Ci-en reply, and a clearly labeled community guide.

That matters because the game's store data is easier to verify than its moment-to-moment feel. We can assess the documented design, content volume, platform situation, and ending structure. We should not invent a personal combat impression or a precise review score.

## The Premise

Iroha Himeno is a disciplinary committee member who meets Lily, a succubus, while on patrol. Lily forms a Pact: win the demons' proxy war as an agent for the succubi, and Iroha's wish will be granted. The bargain makes her part succubus and puts her against the Chosen, humans who made pacts with demons.

The official page frames the story around survival and identity. Iroha may suffer a tragic defeat, and she may eventually have to decide which side of herself is real. That is a stronger hook than a generic "defeat the demons" premise because the transformation is tied to the protagonist's sense of self.

## The Day/Night Loop

The core design is a scheduling RPG:

| Period | Activities | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Daytime | Seminars, jobs, patrols, city exploration | Skills, money, Charm |
| Nighttime | Ligo and the Dream Corridor | Combat training |
| Puzzles | Stat-boosting challenges | Additional growth |
| Story | Chosen encounters | Main plot and defeat routes |

The Japanese DLsite page divides the day further into morning, evening, night, and dream time. That makes the game sound more like a routine to manage than a straight dungeon crawler. Whether that appeals to you depends on whether you enjoy planning activities as much as fighting.

## Combat and Defeat Routes

The official description calls the Chosen battles a battle royale and says each fight is to the death. Mechanically, losing to a Pact-holder does not produce a standard game over. The story enters a defeat route, and Iroha continues looking for a way back to her old life.

That is a meaningful design choice. It makes failure part of the content and reduces the need to reload after every loss. It can also commit the story to a branch, so players who want a clean winning run should keep manual saves before major encounters.

The Dream Corridor provides a separate training space. The Japanese page says the succubi prepared monsters called "sukemon" to teach combat fundamentals.

## Content Volume and Length

DLsite lists roughly 25 hours of play, varying with how many optional scenes are collected. It reports 130 adult scenes with 86 base CG images, plus 42 standing-art scenes with 7 base images, more than 1,400 variations, and 13 costumes plus extras.

Those are substantial figures for an independently developed RPG. They also explain why a completionist run may take much longer than the main story. The game is adults-only, and the adult content is part of the progression rather than a separate gallery-only mode.

## Endings and Replay

There is no official ending count. The developer describes two winning outcomes: memory erasure and memory retention. A Japanese community guide lists three ending events, with the third reached by losing the final battle.

The recollection room is the gallery, and the developer says it cannot be fully unlocked before clearing. That gives a completed run a clear post-game purpose. No official source documents New Game+ or a named true ending.

## Reception and Platform Reality

At the time reviewed, Steam showed 160 user recommendations and a price of S$16.25. DLsite showed 30,648 sales, a 4.78/5 rating from 8,280 ratings, and 115 reviews.

The English release is Steam app 4233960 for Windows. It supports English, Japanese, and Simplified Chinese and includes single-player, Steam Cloud, and Family Sharing. The Japanese PC original is DLsite RJ01241743, with a separate Japanese mobile edition at RJ01398608.

No official English APK, Google Play release, console port, or free full download was verified. Kagura hosts a free v1.05 patch page; its GOG label does not prove a GOG release exists.

## Who It Is For

The game is likely to appeal to players who want a long, systems-heavy adult RPG with a day/night routine, visible stat growth, and defeat branches. It is less suitable for someone who wants a short visual novel, a purely action-driven combat game, or an official English mobile version.

That is a design-based recommendation, not a claim that every player will enjoy the pacing. The 25-hour estimate and large scene count are the best available indicators of commitment.
