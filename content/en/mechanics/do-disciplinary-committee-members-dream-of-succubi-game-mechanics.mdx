## The Mechanics at a Glance

Do Disciplinary Committee Members Dream of Succubi is an adults-only RPG from Milky Way, published in English by Kagura Games. Its mechanics combine a day/night activity loop, turn-based-style RPG encounters, stat growth, equipment purchases, and story branches after defeat.

The [official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4233960/Do_Disciplinary_Committee_Members_Dream_of_Succubi/) is the clearest source for the loop. The Japanese DLsite page adds engine, version, scene counts, and a more granular four-part day. Keeping those sources separate from fan shorthand prevents most mechanical misinformation.

## The Day/Night Loop

The official English description splits play into two broad periods. Daytime is for city exploration and self-improvement. Nighttime is for the succubus town of Ligo and the Dream Corridor.

The Japanese DLsite page expands this into morning, evening, night, and the dream world:

- Morning: disciplinary committee work and public order.
- After school: explore the city and find succubus-related activities.
- Night: enter the training dungeon or a resident's nightmare.
- Dream world: fight, train, and resolve nightmare-related story events.

These descriptions are compatible. The English store gives the high-level rhythm; the Japanese page gives the in-game time blocks. Neither says that every activity is available in every block.

## Combat and the Dream Corridor

The Dream Corridor is the official nighttime training area. The store says Iroha can fight monsters there and polish her combat skills. The Japanese page adds that succubi prepared "sukemon" monsters to teach the fundamentals of battle.

The game's broader combat framing is a battle royale against the Chosen. Those fights are described as battles to the death, but losing to a Pact-holder does not produce a standard game over. The story enters a defeat route instead.

No official source reviewed here publishes damage formulas, elemental weaknesses, or a complete skill list. A combat guide should avoid presenting those as confirmed facts unless it cites the game or developer directly.

## Stats, Charm and Equipment

The official page names Charm and says it rises through work, training, and various activities. Skills come from daytime experiences, and puzzles provide additional stat boosts. Money earned from jobs buys powerful equipment.

That creates a three-part progression loop:

1. Spend time on activities that raise stats or grant skills.
2. Earn money through work.
3. Convert money into equipment for stronger combat.

The exact numbers are not published. There is no official statement that Charm must reach a particular value to unlock an ending, or that one stat is universally more important than another.

## Defeat Routes

The Japanese store describes defeat as content. Iroha loses to another Pact-holder, is humiliated, and continues looking for a chance to restore her former life. The game does not stop at a game-over screen.

This mechanic has two consequences. First, a loss can be explored without losing the save. Second, it can commit the story to an alternate branch for a while, so manual saves remain useful before major fights.

At the final battle, the reported stakes change. A community guide lists the loss as ED03, while the two winning outcomes are selected by a post-victory choice.

## Story Structure and Endings

The developer describes two winning branches: memory erasure and memory retention. The community guide describes three ending events: two after winning and one after losing. The guide also outlines a prologue, chapters 1 through 5, and a final chapter.

No official ending count or true-ending label exists. The recollection room is the gallery, and the developer says it cannot be fully unlocked before clearing.

## Content and Length

DLsite lists roughly 25 hours of play, varying with event collection. It also reports 130 adult scenes with 86 base CG images, 42 standing-art scenes with 7 base images, more than 1,400 variations, and 13 costumes plus extras.

Those figures are content-volume data, not gameplay requirements. A player interested only in the main story will not necessarily see all of them in one run.

## FAQ

**Is combat turn-based?**
The game is an RPG Maker MV RPG with battle encounters. The publisher does not publish a formal turn-order specification on the English page, so describe it as RPG combat rather than asserting an unverified detail.

**What is Charm used for?**
The official page says work, training, and activities raise Charm as Iroha becomes more demonic. No exact ending threshold is published.

**Can I lose without a game over?**
Yes. Losing to a Pact-holder moves the story into a defeat route. The final-battle loss is reported as a distinct ending event.

**How long is the game?**
DLsite lists about 25 hours, depending on how many optional scenes are collected.
