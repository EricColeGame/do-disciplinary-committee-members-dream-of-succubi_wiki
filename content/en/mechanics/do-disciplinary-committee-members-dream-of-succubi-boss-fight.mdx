## How Boss Fights Fit the Story

The [official Steam description](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4233960/Do_Disciplinary_Committee_Members_Dream_of_Succubi/) frames the central conflict as a battle royale. When Iroha fights another Chosen, the battle is "to the death," and the store asks what that toll will take on her and the world.

The Chosen are humans who made pacts with demons. Their identities are hidden, so Iroha must track them down before confronting them. That makes a boss fight both a combat test and a story reveal, not an optional arena challenge.

No official page reviewed here lists boss names, weaknesses, levels, or an exact order. Any guide that presents those details needs a first-party citation or a clearly labeled player report.

## The Two Layers of Combat

The game separates practice from story confrontation:

| Layer | Where it happens | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Training | Ligo and the Dream Corridor at night | Fight monsters and polish combat skills |
| Story duel | Chosen encounters | Advance the proxy war and trigger consequences |
| Defeat branch | After losing to a Pact-holder | Continue through alternate story content |

The Japanese DLsite description adds that Iroha learns combat fundamentals by fighting "sukemon" monsters prepared by the succubi. Those encounters are the low-pressure place to learn how skills, items, and equipment behave.

## Preparation Supported by the Official Pages

The publisher confirms three progression channels that matter before a major fight:

- **Skills:** daytime experiences help Iroha learn new abilities.
- **Equipment:** money earned from jobs buys powerful new equipment.
- **Stats:** puzzles and other activities provide additional growth.

That produces a practical checklist without inventing a required level:

1. Spend work time if your equipment is clearly behind.
2. Attend available seminars and learn new skills.
3. Complete stat-boosting puzzles you have left unfinished.
4. Train in the Dream Corridor until the battle system feels familiar.
5. Save before a story duel so you can choose whether to continue after a loss.

The official page also names Charm as a stat raised by work, training, and other activities. It does not state that a specific Charm value is required to defeat a particular boss.

## What Happens If You Lose

The Japanese DLsite page is explicit: losing to a Pact-holder does not cause a game over. The story shifts into a defeat route. Iroha is humiliated, but she can use the power she gains from adult encounters to endure and look for a chance to recover her former life.

This is a designed branch, not a soft lock. It still changes the immediate direction of the story, so save before the fight if you want to keep both options open. The existence of a defeat route also means there is no need to reload in panic the first time a Chosen beats you.

At the final battle, the loss branch is more consequential. A Japanese community guide lists losing there as ED03, while winning leads to a post-victory choice between two other ending events.

## Final-Battle Preparation

The final battle is the one encounter with a clearly reported ending split. The safe preparation is less about a secret build and more about preserving access to the branches:

- Keep a manual save before the fight.
- Bring the equipment and skills you have actually unlocked.
- Win once and record the result of each post-victory option.
- Reload and lose once if you want to verify the community-reported loss ending.

The developer's Ci-en reply describes the two winning outcomes as memory erasure and memory retention. It does not say that a particular combat style selects one.

## What Not to Believe

No official source reviewed here supports a boss-specific weakness table, an auto-lose command, an easy mode, or a hidden ending triggered by arriving with a particular costume. The recollection room is real, but the developer says it cannot be fully unlocked before clearing, so gallery completion is not boss-fight preparation.

Community advice can still be useful, but separate it from verified mechanics. A player report might describe a strategy that worked in version 1.11; it is not proof of a universal requirement.

## Versions and Performance

The English release is Steam app 4233960 for Windows. The Japanese PC original is DLsite RJ01241743. DLsite lists version 1.11 as the then-current PC build in the page reviewed for this article, with fixes for several event and progression bugs.

The Steam page warns that lag may occur when loading menus or maps and suggests closing other programs. If a command seems unresponsive during a boss fight, that performance warning is a more plausible explanation than a hidden input mechanic.

## FAQ

**Who are the bosses in Do Disciplinary Committee Members Dream of Succubi?**
The main story confrontations are with the Chosen, humans who made pacts with demons. The official pages do not publish a complete named boss list.

**Is there a game over if I lose a boss fight?**
The Japanese store says a loss to a Pact-holder moves the story into a defeat route instead of ending the run.

**How should I prepare for the final boss?**
Keep equipment and skills current, use the Dream Corridor for practice, and save before the fight. No official stat threshold is published.

**Can I see every finale branch with one save?**
Yes, if you reload. Win and test both post-victory options, then reload and lose for the reported third ending.
