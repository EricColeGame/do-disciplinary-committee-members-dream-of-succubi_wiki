## The Official Combat Loop

Combat in Do Disciplinary Committee Members Dream of Succubi is tied to the day/night schedule. The [official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4233960/Do_Disciplinary_Committee_Members_Dream_of_Succubi/) says daytime activities teach skills and earn money for equipment. At night, Iroha travels to the succubus town of Ligo and uses the Dream Corridor to fight monsters and polish her combat skills.

Puzzles add stat boosts on top of those gains. The Chosen then turn that preparation into story progress, because defeating other Pact-holders is the central objective of the demons' proxy war.

| Phase | Confirmed activity | Combat benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Daytime | Patrol, jobs, seminars, puzzles | Money, skills, stat growth, Charm |
| Night in Ligo | Dream Corridor training | Monster fights and combat practice |
| Chosen duel | Story battle against a Pact-holder | Main progression or a defeat route |

## Is Combat Turn-Based?

The official English store page calls the genre Adventure and describes combat without publishing a formal turn-order specification. The Japanese DLsite page identifies the engine as **RPG Maker MV 1.6.1**.

RPG Maker MV is commonly associated with menu-driven, turn-based-style battles. That makes it a reasonable expectation, but it is engine context rather than a publisher statement about every encounter. The safe description is that this is an RPG Maker RPG with battle encounters, not a real-time action game.

No official damage formula, enemy stat table, or skill list was found in the sources reviewed here.

## The Dream Corridor

The Dream Corridor is the named nighttime training area in Ligo. The store describes it as the place to fight monsters and improve combat skills.

The Japanese DLsite page adds that the succubi prepared training monsters called "sukemon" to teach the fundamentals of battle. That makes the corridor the natural place to learn how skills, items, and equipment behave before a major Chosen encounter.

No official source states a required level for a particular story fight. Preparation advice should therefore focus on the systems the game documents: earn equipment money through work, learn skills through daytime experience, complete stat-boosting puzzles, and use the corridor for practice.

## Chosen Duels and Defeat Routes

The official premise says fights against the Chosen are to the death. The Japanese DLsite description then explains the mechanical consequence of losing: the game does not show a standard game-over screen. The story enters a defeat route, and Iroha continues after the humiliation while looking for a way back to her former life.

That is a designed branch. It can be explored deliberately, but it also changes the immediate direction of the story. Keep a manual save before a major Chosen fight if you want to preserve both the win and loss paths.

| Outcome | What follows | Is it a final ending? |
|---|---|---|
| Win a training battle | Continue training or the current dungeon | No |
| Lose a training battle | Follow the scene or retry as the game allows | No |
| Lose to a Chosen mid-story | Defeat route, then continued progression | Usually no |
| Win the final battle | Post-victory choice between two reported ending events | Yes |
| Lose the final battle | Community-reported ED03 | Yes, per third-party guide |

## Boss Preparation

There is no official boss guide. The documented preparation loop is still useful:

- Work jobs to buy equipment.
- Attend seminars and learn skills.
- Solve puzzles for stat boosts.
- Train in the Dream Corridor.
- Save before a major Chosen encounter.

The developer's Ci-en reply describes the two winning outcomes in terms of memory, not a combat build. It does not say that a specific weapon, Charm value, or costume selects one.

At the final battle, the practical goal is to preserve access to the branches. Save before the fight, win once to test both post-victory options, then reload and lose if you want to verify the reported third ending.
