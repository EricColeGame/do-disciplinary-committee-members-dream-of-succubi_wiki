## Is There a Farewell OST?

No official Do Disciplinary Committee Members Dream of Succubi farewell OST, ending-theme single, or named farewell track was found on the Steam, Kagura Games, or DLsite pages reviewed here.

The game includes music, and its ending structure includes two developer-described winning branches plus a community-reported loss ending. Those facts do not prove that a separate farewell song exists, let alone that it has a published title.

## Why the Phrase Sounds Plausible

A story about memory, identity, and alternate outcomes naturally invites the word "farewell." Fans may use it for an ending montage, a final scene, or a piece heard during the credits.

That is an interpretation, not a product listing. Without a composer credit, track list, or official release page, an article cannot turn the mood into a confirmed song.

The [official Steam page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4233960/Do_Disciplinary_Committee_Members_Dream_of_Succubi/) does host trailers, which are an authorised way to hear some of the game's audio. A trailer cue is still not evidence of a track called "Farewell."

## What the Endings Actually Establish

The developer's August 17, 2025 Ci-en reply describes two winning outcomes: memory erasure and memory retention. The memory-erasure branch returns the story close to its prior status quo. The memory-retention branch keeps Iroha's experiences and implies a different personal aftermath.

A Japanese community guide lists three ending events: ED01 「現代に生きるサキュバス」 and ED02 「帰ってきた日常」 after winning, plus ED03 「永遠の悪夢」 after losing. That guide is third-party.

None of those labels is a music track title. The Japanese ending names should not be repurposed as song names.

## Official Music Information

DLsite tags the game with **音楽あり**, which means music is included. The listing reviewed here does not provide:

- An ending-theme single.

The absence is important. It means the honest answer is "not documented," not "probably called something."

## What Not to Claim

Do not claim that:

- A farewell theme is officially named.
- The ending credits have a licensed vocal song.
- The memory-erasure and memory-retention branches each have a separate theme.
- The community ending labels are music titles.
